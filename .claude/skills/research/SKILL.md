# research

Every claim carries a source (video id, timestamp or comment count). Numbers you did not measure do not exist.

## Steps
1. **Baseline first.** Post views vs the channel median is the only honest measure of "worked".
   `views / channel_median = outlier x`. Below 1.5x is not an outlier, say so.
2. **Read the video, do not just transcribe it.** Pull frames every 2s, build contact sheets, read them in order
   against the transcript. Log: exact hook words + what is on screen at that second, first cut, cut rhythm,
   text style, b-roll source, structure, CTA. A breakdown from transcript alone is a guess and must be labelled one.
3. **Sound.** If the agent cannot hear, say "inferred from cut points" and never claim an SFX you did not detect.
4. **Comments.** Top comments tell you what the audience quoted and argued about - that is usually the next video.
5. **Output a table, not an essay.** One row per section: `timestamp | beat | what is on screen | why it holds | stealable pattern`.
6. **Turn findings into candidates, not rules.** Append to `brand/rules.md` under **Proposed**. They only lock when
   the creator says "lock it". A rule needs two independent videos of evidence.
7. **Never** copy wording or footage. Structure and patterns are borrowable; a creator's sentences and clips are not.

## Done when
- [ ] every row has a timestamp and a frame reference
- [ ] `outlier x` is stated as a number
- [ ] proposed rules are in the Proposed table, dated, with the video id as evidence
- [ ] a "what to skip" list exists - what worked for them that would fail for this creator's audience
